Track Day Cut

An unofficial short-film-style trailer edit built from track-day Porsche footage, built around pacing, sound design and cinematic color.

Start a Similar Project
Tools Used
Premiere Pro After Effects
Overview

Treating raw footage like a short film

A short, unofficial trailer-style edit built from track-day footage of a Porsche — treated less like a car video and more like a short film with a beginning, a rise, and a payoff.

Objective & Problem

Fast cuts and engine noise aren't a story

Raw track-day footage is usually just fast cuts and engine noise. The goal was to turn that raw footage into something with real narrative tension — the kind of trailer that builds anticipation, even though it's just a car on a track.

Solution

Borrowing pacing from film trailers

A pacing structure borrowed from film trailers — a slow, moody build-up, a rhythm shift at the midpoint, and a punchy final sequence — supported by sound design that layered engine notes with a proper score instead of leaving raw audio in.

Design Process

Cut first for pacing, then for polish

Cut a rough assembly first to lock the pacing, then graded the footage for a cinematic, slightly desaturated look, and finished with a full sound pass — engine layering, ambient track sound and a score.

Final Design

Under 90 seconds, treated like a real release

A sub-90-second trailer cut with color grading, sound design and title cards treated like a real film release, not a social clip.

Outcome

Proof of range beyond quick reels

A piece that shows range beyond short-form social content — the same storytelling instinct applies whether the format is 15 seconds or a minute and a half.
